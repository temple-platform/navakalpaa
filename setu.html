Simplifying temple operations and devotee services.

The application

Screens from Setu.

These screens are from Setu while it is in development: a sample public page, a staff booking, and the temple dashboard. The temple name and place on the public page are a depiction. Prices shown are sample seva prices, not a price for Setu. This website does not take bookings or payments.

The work

Everyday temple work these screens address.

  • Seeing the sevas

    Devotees need a clear list of what a temple offers, with a price and a way to start.

  • Recording a booking

    Staff need the chosen sevas, the date and the time together, and a payment step before the booking is finished.

  • Seeing the day

    The temple needs one view of what was collected and booked, with the catalog, history, profile and staff close by.

What is on screen

What Setu shows.

The notes below describe only what is visible in the screens above.

A public page for devotees

The sample page has a place for the temple’s name and area. On this picture those words are a depiction. The page says it is an official temple service, and says payments go straight to the temple. Each seva shows a price, that it is available all days, and a Book button.

A booking for staff

A new booking moves through Search, Seva, Pay and Done. On the Seva step, staff select one or more sevas, set the date and a time slot, and proceed to payment. The same screen has Dashboard, New, History and Profile.

A dashboard for the temple

The admin view shows what was collected and how many bookings were made today and this month. A recent booking can be marked paid. From the same screen the temple opens the areas below.

Seva catalog
Add, edit and price sevas.
Booking history
All bookings and receipts.
Temple profile
The temple’s name, UPI and timings.
Staff
The temple’s counter staff and poojaris.

The staff area on the dashboard is for the temple’s own people. The picture shows that label. It is not a public service for booking a poojari.

Still being built

How a seva is meant to move.

Setu is in development. This is the path it is being built around. This website does not take a booking or a payment.

  1. 1

    Scan

    A devotee scans the temple’s QR code. Looking through the sevas does not need an app install.

  2. 2

    Choose a seva

    They see the sevas and prices, and pick a date and time when a slot is offered.

  3. 3

    Sign in and pay

    Sign-in uses a phone OTP. Payment is being built for cash at the counter and for UPI.

  4. 4

    Ticket

    A QR ticket is shown on the screen and is meant to be used once.

  5. 5

    Counter

    Staff can find a devotee, record a walk-in, confirm a payment, and scan the ticket.

  6. 6

    Poojari

    The poojari sees that day’s sevas and can mark one as performed.

Who it is for

Four roles inside the temple.

Setu is being built in Telugu and English, so the same screens can be read in either language. The poojari here is one of the temple’s own roles, not a person a visitor books from a public list.

  • Devotee

    Browses sevas from the temple QR code, books, pays, and can look back at their bookings.

  • Counter staff

    Finds a devotee by phone, records a walk-in, confirms cash or UPI, and scans tickets.

  • Temple admin

    Keeps the temple profile, including its name, deity, address, UPI and timings, plus the seva catalog, prices and staff.

  • Poojari

    Sees the day’s confirmed sevas and the devotee’s name and gotram for the sankalpa, then records that a seva was performed.
